Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We are preparing a manuscript provisionally entitled “When Does a Second Detector Help? Checkpoint-Sensitive Safety-Object Complementarity under PPE Domain Shift” for possible consideration as an original research paper in *Automation in Construction*.

The manuscript examines a specific methodological problem in automated construction-site PPE monitoring: whether activating a second, domain-specialized detector actually recovers missed safety violations, and at what increase in false detections. We analyze six previously trained YOLO11m checkpoints across two harmonized PPE domains and three matched random seeds, while explicitly separating final-epoch checkpoint results, validation-selected checkpoint sensitivity, genuine joint non-maximum-suppression fusion and a nondeployable ground-truth-privileged oracle. The development results show checkpoint-dependent object-level complementarity, sparse opportunities for effective second-expert invocation and a substantial false-detection burden. We do not claim deployment-ready PPE safety or measured energy savings.

The analysis is a candidate methodological limits/robustness contribution, rather than the invention of a new model-cascade algorithm. We transparently identify the relationship of this study to a separate previous YOLO11m cross-domain reliability research track, whose individual-detector test data and some baselines were already examined. The current selective-fusion analyses are development-validation results, not independently corroborated field claims. Dataset licensing, source-group dependence, retrospective checkpoint-selection effects and rare negative-class support are expressly disclosed.
